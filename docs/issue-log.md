# Operational Issue Log — Example

This is a fictional example showing how I structure operational exceptions for visibility and follow-through.

| ID | Issue | Category | Priority | Owner | Status | Next Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OPS-01 | Toll receipt missing for VAN-04 trip | Expense Documentation | Medium | Operations | Open | Request supporting receipt and validate claim |
| OPS-02 | Repeated loading delay on South route | Service Delivery | High | Operations Coordinator | Investigating | Review dispatch/loading handoff and timing |
| OPS-03 | VAN-04 tyre repair required | Maintenance | Medium | Fleet Lead | Closed | Repair completed; monitor recurrence |
| OPS-04 | Expense amount requires verification | Expense Control | Medium | Operations | Review | Compare claim with trip record and supporting evidence |

## Issue Management Rules

1. Record exceptions when identified rather than relying on memory or chat messages.
2. Assign a clear owner.
3. Separate priority from status.
4. Record the next action so an issue can move forward.
5. Review open high-priority issues frequently.
6. Look for recurring causes across closed issues and convert them into improvement actions.
