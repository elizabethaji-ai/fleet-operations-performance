# Fleet Operations KPI Framework

This framework shows how operational records can be converted into management information. All examples are based on fictional portfolio data.

| KPI | Purpose | Example Calculation | Management Question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trip Completion Rate | Measures service execution | Completed trips / Total trips | Are planned movements being completed? |
| On-Time / Exception Rate | Highlights service disruption | Delayed trips / Total trips | Where are operational delays occurring? |
| Cost per Trip | Tracks operating efficiency | Total operating cost / Trips | Are trip costs changing materially? |
| Cost per Kilometre | Normalises costs by activity | Total operating cost / Distance | Which vehicles/routes are comparatively expensive? |
| Documentation Compliance | Measures expense-control quality | Complete receipts / Expense records | Are claims supported by required evidence? |
| Expense Review Rate | Highlights reconciliation workload | Records requiring review / Total records | How much manual follow-up is being created? |
| Maintenance Cost by Vehicle | Identifies asset cost patterns | Maintenance spend grouped by vehicle | Is a vehicle becoming disproportionately expensive? |
| Vehicle Utilisation | Shows distribution of activity | Trips or km by vehicle / fleet total | Is workload balanced across the fleet? |

## Review Cadence

**Daily:** delivery exceptions, missing documentation and urgent vehicle issues.  
**Weekly:** utilisation, trip completion, unresolved expenses and recurring operational issues.  
**Monthly:** cost trends, maintenance patterns, expense compliance and improvement actions.

## Decision Use

The objective of the KPI set is not reporting for reporting's sake. Each measure should trigger a management question or follow-up action. For example, a rising expense-review rate can indicate unclear submission requirements, while repeated maintenance costs on one vehicle may justify preventive maintenance or an asset decision.
